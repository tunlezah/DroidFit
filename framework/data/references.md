# References

Citation keys used throughout the framework, the exercise catalogue's `evidence_keys`
fields, and `project_memory/research_summary.md`.

**Rules for the building agent:**
1. Never invent a citation. If you cannot find the source, the claim does not ship.
2. Never cite a paper you have not at least read the abstract of.
3. When you add a key, add it here with enough detail to find the paper again: authors,
   year, title, journal, and a DOI or PMID.
4. Keys are lowercase, `firstauthor` + `year` + a short tag, and are never reused.

## Systematic reviews and meta-analyses

### `chen2024nma` — the primary source for programme design
Chen X, He H, Xie K, Zhang L, Cao C. **Effects of various exercise types on visceral
adipose tissue in individuals with overweight and obesity: A systematic review and network
meta-analysis of 84 randomized controlled trials.** *Obesity Reviews*, 2024;25(3):e13666.
DOI: 10.1111/obr.13666. PMID: 38031812. (Epub 2023-11-30.)

84 RCTs, 4,836 participants. Aerobic exercise at ≥ moderate intensity, resistance
training, aerobic + resistance, and HIIT were all beneficial for VAT. SUCRA ranking placed
vigorous-intensity aerobic exercise and HIIT highest for VAT, weight, total body fat, BMI,
waist circumference and subcutaneous fat. Resistance training was least effective, and
improved VAT in males and those below 40% body fat but not in females or those at or above
40%.

### `wang2021pilates` — the constraint on how Pilates is presented
Wang Y, Chen Z, Wu Z, Ye X, Xu X. **Pilates for Overweight or Obesity: A Meta-Analysis.**
*Frontiers in Physiology*, 2021;12:643455. DOI: 10.3389/fphys.2021.643455. PMID: 33776797.

11 RCTs, 393 participants. Significant reductions in body mass (MD −2.40 kg, 95% CI −4.04
to −0.77), BMI (MD −1.17 kg/m², 95% CI −1.85 to −0.50) and body fat percentage (MD −4.22%,
95% CI −6.44 to −2.01). **No significant effect on waist circumference** (MD −2.65 cm, 95%
CI −6.84 to +1.55) or lean mass. Larger effects in obesity-only populations and in
interventions over 10 weeks. Protocols: 30–90 min, 3–6×/week, 8–24 weeks; 77% female.

### `ismail2012` — aerobic versus resistance for VAT
Ismail I, Keating SE, Baker MK, Johnson NA. **A systematic review and meta-analysis of the
effect of aerobic vs. resistance exercise training on visceral fat.** *Obesity Reviews*,
2012;13(1):68–91. DOI: 10.1111/j.1467-789X.2011.00931.x. PMID: 21951360.

Significant pooled effect for aerobic exercise versus control; no significant effect for
progressive resistance training versus control. The direct aerobic-versus-resistance
comparison favoured aerobic without reaching significance. Also the source for the finding
that low-to-moderate intensity preferentially reduces abdominal subcutaneous fat while
higher intensity is needed to reduce visceral fat comparably.

### `sultana2023` — HIIT versus MICT
Sultana RN, Sabag A, Keating SE, Johnson NA, et al. **Effect of High-Intensity Interval
Training vs. Moderate-Intensity Continuous Training on Fat Loss and Cardiorespiratory
Fitness in the Young and Middle-Aged: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.**
PMC10048683 (2023).

HIIT's effect on fat loss is similar to or better than MICT, moderated by age, obesity
status, duration, frequency and interval type. Head-to-head comparisons show no consistent
statistically significant difference between modalities for body fat.

## Guidelines

### `who2020` — the weekly volume frame
Bull FC, Al-Ansari SS, Biddle S, Borodulin K, Buman MP, Cardon G, et al. **World Health
Organization 2020 guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour.** *British
Journal of Sports Medicine*, 2020;54(24):1451–1462. DOI: 10.1136/bjsports-2020-102955.
PMID: 33239350. PMC7719906.

Adults: 150–300 min/week moderate-intensity **or** 75–150 min/week vigorous-intensity
aerobic activity, or an equivalent combination; plus muscle-strengthening activity at
moderate or greater intensity involving all major muscle groups on 2 or more days a week.
The 2020 revision introduced upper bounds where the 2010 guidance gave only minimums.

### `donnelly2009` — ACSM position stand on activity for weight management
Donnelly JE, Blair SN, Jakicic JM, Manore MM, Rankin JW, Smith BK; American College of
Sports Medicine. **Appropriate physical activity intervention strategies for weight loss
and prevention of weight regain for adults.** *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*,
2009;41(2):459–471. DOI: 10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181949333.

150–250 min/week of moderate activity prevents weight gain and produces modest weight
loss. Clinically significant weight loss is reported above 250 min/week. Weight
maintenance after loss is better above 250 min/week, on cross-sectional and prospective
evidence.

### `acsm2021` — exercise prescription reference
American College of Sports Medicine. **ACSM's Guidelines for Exercise Testing and
Prescription**, 11th edition. Wolters Kluwer, 2021. ISBN 9781975150181.

Source for the FITT-VP framework (Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type, Volume, Progression),
warm-up and cool-down structure, progression principles, and pre-participation screening
guidance behind the app's safety notice.

## Protocols and primary trials

### `helgerud2007` — the Norwegian 4×4
Helgerud J, Høydal K, Wang E, Karlsen T, Berg P, Bjerkaas M, et al. **Aerobic high-intensity
intervals improve VO2max more than moderate training.** *Medicine & Science in Sports &
Exercise*, 2007;39(4):665–671. DOI: 10.1249/mss.0b013e3180304570. PMID: 17414804.

4 × 4 min at 90–95% HRmax with 3 min active recovery at ~70% HRmax, 3×/week for 8 weeks,
produced substantially greater VO₂max improvement than volume-matched moderate continuous
training. The origin of the widely used "4×4" protocol; developed at NTNU and studied
extensively by the Cardiac Exercise Research Group.

### `ozturk2025reformer` — reformer Pilates RCT
**Effects of reformer Pilates on body composition, strength, and psychosomatic factors in
overweight and obese women: a randomized controlled trial.** *Scientific Reports*, 2025.
DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-09683-8.

Reports body-composition, strength and psychosomatic improvements from a reformer Pilates
intervention in overweight and obese women. The only reformer-specific source cited;
treated as single-trial evidence, not as a basis for strong claims.

## Reference data

### `pacompendium2024` — MET values
Herrmann SD, Willis EA, Ainsworth BE, Barreira TV, Hastert M, Kracht CL, et al.
**2024 Adult Compendium of Physical Activities: A third update of the energy costs of human
activities.** *Journal of Sport and Health Science*, 2024;13(1):6–12.
DOI: 10.1016/j.jshs.2023.10.010. Data at https://pacompendium.com/adult-compendium/

Source of every MET value in `framework/data/met_values.json`, cited by activity code.
Contains **no reformer-Pilates entry** — see A-0004.

### `rogers2025zone2` — Zone 2 definitional variability
**Zone 2 Intensity: A Critical Comparison of Individual Variability in Different Submaximal
Exercise Intensity Boundaries.** PMC11986187 (2025).

Compares Zone 2 boundary definitions and their individual variability. Establishes that
Z2 corresponds to roughly 72–82% HRmax, 1.5–2.5 mmol/L lactate, or 60–75% FTP depending on
the model used, and that lactate-anchored definitions vary more between individuals than
percentage-of-max definitions.

## Sources deliberately not used

- **Fitness influencer content, YouTube, blog posts.** No editorial standard, and the
  exact place where claims like "targets belly fat" originate.
- **Single small RCTs presented as definitive.** Used only where nothing stronger exists
  (`ozturk2025reformer`), and labelled as such.
- **Company-published research on its own product.** Conflicted.
- **Anything paywalled that could not be read.** Where only an abstract was accessible,
  this document cites the abstract's findings and does not claim more. `chen2024nma`,
  `wang2021pilates` and `ismail2012` were read at abstract level via PubMed and PMC; full
  texts were behind paywalls. This limit is stated here rather than hidden.
